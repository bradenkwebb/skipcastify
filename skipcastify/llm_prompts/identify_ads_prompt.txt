WHAT TO FLAG:

1. Standard ads and sponsor reads: product pitches, promo codes, CTAs ("visit X.com", "use code Y"). URL-based CTAs are a hard signal — any segment containing "go to X.com", "learn more at X.com/Y", "visit X slash Y", or a vanity URL is advertisement content; include that segment and its immediately preceding product-setup in the same span.

2. Sponsored content inserts: a clip promoting another podcast, service, or show that is explicitly paid for. Signs of a paid insert: "brought to you in partnership with [company]", "an original podcast from [company]", "sponsored by", a partner-branded URL or app link (e.g. "listen on the iHeart radio app", "visit brand.com/podcast"), or a sponsor sign-off immediately after. A brief 5-second transition line is fine to keep; flag content inserts that run 15 seconds or longer.
   NOT a paid insert: a host saying "for more on this, listen to [show from the same network]" or "available wherever you get your podcasts" — when there is no explicit sponsor attribution, no external/partner URL, and no promo code. This is editorial cross-promotion. Do not flag it.

3. Ad breaks: when you have already identified TWO separate ad spans within roughly 2 minutes of each other, treat the content between those two spans as part of the same continuous ad break — unless it is clearly substantive editorial content (e.g., a full interview answer, a news report, at least 15 seconds of genuine editorial speech). A short tease, hook, or product-promo fragment between two identified ads is almost certainly part of the break; output a single merged span from the start of the first to the end of the last. Important: this rule applies only to content between two spans you have already flagged, not to content that precedes the first ad in a section.

4. Boundary completeness: when boundaries are uncertain, usually expand the span outward:
   - Expand forward to include the CTA: end_ms must be set PAST the segment containing the URL, promo code, or sign-off — not at the segment before it. If you know a URL CTA is coming, include the segment that actually contains "Go to X.com" or "Use code Y" inside the span. The CTA segment is part of the ad, not a segment that follows the ad.
   - Expand backward for cut-off sentences: if a segment ends with an incomplete sentence ("Back for season two of...") and the next segment completes that sentence with sponsor language, the incomplete segment is part of the ad — pull start_ms back to include it.
   - Expand backward for teasers: an opening hook ("Guess who's back?") or problem-setup immediately before a sponsor attribution is part of that ad unit.
   A span that stops before the product's URL/CTA, or that starts after the product's tease, is incomplete.

5. Branded narrative content: ads sometimes disguise themselves as a short podcast preview or branded story ("On this episode of [Show], we explore...") where the product appears shortly after. The narrative framing is part of the ad — flag the whole thing from its fictional "episode intro" through the product name and close.

6. Problem-setup → product-reveal pattern (expand in BOTH directions): many ads open with a relatable problem, symptom list, or hook before naming the product, and may not reveal a brand or CTA until the very end.
   - Forward: if you've flagged a problem-setup segment, the product-reveal, benefits, and CTA in the following segments are part of the same ad unit — include them even if they sound more descriptive than promotional.
   - Backward: if you've flagged a brand mention or a CTA/URL, look back through the immediately preceding contiguous segments and pull start_ms back to include the entire lead-in that builds to it — the problem/symptom framing, "talk to your doctor"-style advice, or storytelling hook.
   - Pre-roll position: a promotional block at the very start of a section — before the show's name, theme music, or the host's greeting — is very likely a pre-roll ad. When such an opening block ends in a brand or CTA, flag it from the first segment (start_ms at or near 0 / the section start). Position at the top of the episode is itself a strong ad signal.
   An ad's lead-in can sound like a neutral consumer tip or public-health message (listing memory symptoms, explaining a medical condition, describing a relatable frustration) right up until the brand is named. A HARD CTA at the end is what signals the whole preceding contiguous block was an ad: an external URL, a promo code, an app-download prompt, or "visit / go to / use code X". Apply this backward expansion ONLY when such a hard CTA is present.
   Critically, this does NOT loosen the editorial guard: a bare recommendation to check out another show ("listen to the X Podcast", "available wherever you get your podcasts") with no URL, no promo code, and no sponsor attribution is NOT a CTA — it is editorial cross-promotion and must stay (see Examples C and E). Ordinary news reports and interview answers are never ad lead-ins, even when they mention a company or product.
